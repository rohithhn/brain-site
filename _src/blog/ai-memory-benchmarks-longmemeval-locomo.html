LongMemEval and LoCoMo explained: how to benchmark AI memory

Every AI memory product quotes the same two benchmarks. Here's what LongMemEval and LoCoMo actually measure, how their scores work, why two "LoCoMo scores" can mean different things, and eight questions to ask before you trust any number, including ours.

By Rohith, Chandan and Sushmita

Why benchmark AI memory at all?

Because "it remembers" is easy to demo and hard to prove. A memory layer can look great on three hand-picked questions and quietly miss the fourth. Public benchmarks put hundreds of questions with known answers in front of it, so the miss rate shows.

Two benchmarks have become the standard for long-term memory: LongMemEval (published at ICLR 2025) and LoCoMo (published at ACL 2024). Almost every memory product quotes one or both.

What is LongMemEval?

500 questions about long chat histories between a user and an assistant, each with the answer hidden somewhere in dozens or hundreds of past sessions.

VariantHistory per questionFits in a context window?
LongMemEval-SAbout 50 sessions, roughly 500 turnsIn large models, yes
LongMemEval-MAbout 500 sessions, around 1.27 million tokensNo current model

The questions cover six kinds of memory work:

Facts the user stated

"What's my dog's name?" from one session.

Facts the assistant said

Recalling the assistant's own earlier answer.

Preferences

Using what the user likes when answering.

Knowledge updates

The fact changed later; use the newest version.

Temporal reasoning

"How long after X did Y happen?"

Multi-session

Combining evidence spread across several chats.

Thirty of the 500 are abstention questions: the answer isn't in the history, and the right response is to say so. They catch systems that guess confidently.

What is the LoCoMo benchmark?

10 very long conversations between two people, with 1,986 questions about them.

CategoryQuestionsWhat it tests
Single-hop841One fact from one turn
Temporal321When something happened
Multi-hop282Combining facts across sessions
Open-domain96Common-sense inference about the people
Adversarial446Questions with no valid answer

Most published results score the first four categories, 1,540 questions, and leave the adversarial category out. One trap: the dataset labels categories with numbers that don't follow the order papers list them in, so it's easy to report "multi-hop" with the wrong questions. Check the mapping against the data itself.

Each LoCoMo conversation is about 20,000 tokens, so it fits in any modern model's context. That makes "give the model the whole conversation" a fair and important baseline.

Retrieval recall vs answer accuracy: what's the difference?

Recall measures the search. Accuracy measures the answer.

Retrieval recall (e.g. recall@5)Answer accuracy (QA)
Question askedWas the evidence in the top results?Was the final answer correct?
Needs an LLM?NoYes: one to answer, one to judge
Typical numbersOften high 90sLower, and depends on the models used

Both are useful. Recall tells you the memory found the right material; accuracy tells you the whole system worked. Just never compare one product's recall with another's accuracy.

How do LLM judges work, and can you trust them?

A second model compares each answer with the gold answer and labels it right or wrong.

LLM judges are lenient on wording (that's the point: "May 7th" and "7 May" are both right), but they're still models with their own biases. Two simple checks make a judged score much more credible: grade the same answers with a judge from a different model family and report how often they agree, and publish every answer and verdict so anyone can re-grade them.

8 questions to ask before you trust a memory benchmark claim

Ask these of every number, ours included.

  1. Recall or accuracy? Which kind of score is it?
  2. Which variant and categories? LongMemEval-S or M? LoCoMo with or without adversarial questions?
  3. Which models answered and judged? A stronger reader raises the score without changing the memory.
  4. Compared with what? Is there a full-context baseline on the same model, or only other products' self-reported numbers?
  5. At what cost? Tokens per question and price, not just accuracy.
  6. Is the judge checked? A second judge from another model family, with agreement reported.
  7. Was it tuned on the test? Settings chosen on a held-out half are more honest than settings tuned on the whole set.
  8. Can you verify it? Published harness, per-question answers and verdicts.

Full context beats memory on LoCoMo when the conversation fits, and we say so: memory reaches 97% of that score with 18% of the tokens. On LongMemEval-M the history doesn't fit at all, and memory is the only option. The harness, every answer and every verdict are in our repository. Frequently asked questions

What is LongMemEval?

A benchmark of 500 questions about long user–assistant chat histories, in an S size (~50 sessions per question) and an M size (~500 sessions, ~1.27M tokens).

What is the LoCoMo benchmark?

10 very long two-person conversations with 1,986 questions in five categories; most results score four categories, 1,540 questions, with an LLM judge.

What's the difference between recall and answer accuracy?

Recall: was the evidence found? Accuracy: was the final answer right? Don't compare one with the other.

Are scores comparable between products?

Only with the same answering model, judge, question set and categories. Otherwise read them as direction, not a precise ranking.

What is a good LongMemEval score?

Strong memory systems published in 2026 cluster in the low-to-mid 90s on LongMemEval-S. Ours is 94.2% (93.4% with a GPT judge).
